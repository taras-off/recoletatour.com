Recoleta Cemetery: Guided Tour, Audio Guide or On Your Own?

Three ways to see the same 4,600 vaults. One is free, one is flexible, one has a human. Here's how they really compare.

By EugeneUpdated September 20267-minute read
Buy the audio guide Tickets & hours Book a guided tour

You can walk into Recoleta Cemetery with nothing but a ticket, and thousands do. Most of them find Eva Perón, photograph a few angels, get lost for a while and leave after forty minutes, having stood within fifty metres of the strangest stories in Argentina without hearing one. The cemetery doesn't explain itself. So the real question isn't whether to get some guidance, but which kind.

There are three honest options: a live guided tour (free in Spanish, paid in English), a self-guided audio guide like TouringBee's, or your own two feet and a map. This is a fair comparison of all three — we sell one of them, and we'll still tell you when the others are the better choice.

Option 1: A guided tour

The free City tours (Spanish)

Every hour a City guide leads a free walk from the main gate — weekdays from 10:00 to 16:00, weekends and holidays from 9:00. They know the cemetery inside out and they give you what a first visit most needs: a chronology, a route through the maze, and names for the faces. If you speak Spanish, this is excellent value for nothing. It's suspended for funerals and weather, and there is no English departure, whatever the person at the hostel told you.

Paid tours in English

Small-group walks in English run several times a day and most include the non-resident entry ticket, which takes some of the sting out of the price. A good guide is a pleasure: they read the doors for you, answer questions, and know which vaults have a spiral staircase visible through the glass. The trade-offs are the ones every group tour has — a fixed departure, a fixed pace, ninety minutes in a huddle of fifteen, and a guide's-eye view of Evita's lane at the worst hour of the day. Choose this if you learn best from a person, you want to ask things, or you're happy to trade freedom for structure. Book the first morning departure.

Option 2: A self-guided audio guide

An audio guide is a tour without the group. You download it in advance, walk in when you like, and start each story yourself when you reach the tomb. TouringBee's Recoleta tour has 22 stops on an offline map — from the portico to the Argentine Nostradamus — each a recording of three to five minutes written by a professional historian. The map matters more here than almost anywhere: mobile signal dies between the marble walls, and a route that works offline is the difference between finding the caretaker's tomb and giving up.

What you lose is the human: nobody to ask, no answer to “what's that?” about the vault that isn't on the route. What you gain is Recoleta at 9:00 with nobody in Evita's lane, the freedom to spend twenty minutes at Rufina's door and skip the vice-president, and stories you can replay. It's narrated in English and Spanish, one payment covers a year, and the whole thing costs less than the entry ticket. It doesn't include the ticket — nothing but the official site does.

Choose this if you're an independent traveller, you're photographing, you're with children, you have limited time, or you just want to hear the stories without standing under someone's umbrella.

Option 3: On your own

Free, unlimited, and the choice of most people — which is why most people see so little. Recoleta rewards knowledge: a tomb is a lovely piece of stone until you know that the girl carved on the door supposedly woke in her coffin, and then it's unforgettable. Signage inside is sparse. There's a paper map at the gate on good days. Wandering does have real pleasures: you'll find neglected vaults with spiral stairs going down into the dark, a cat on a dome, a stained-glass ceiling nobody photographs. Just be honest about what you'll get: atmosphere, yes; the stories, no.

Choose this if it's your second visit, you've read up beforehand (our route guide is free), or you only have half an hour and want Evita and the light.

The best answer: combine them

If you have the time, do two laps. Take a tour first — the free Spanish one if you can, a paid English one if not — for the chronology and to learn the grid. Then go round again with the audio guide, in the reverse order if you like, and stop where the group didn't let you. The second lap is where the cemetery actually gets under your skin. And if you only have one lap, make it the audio guide at 9:00.

What it costs, honestly

For two people on a paid English tour: US$30–70 including tickets, 90 minutes, one morning. For two people with an audio guide: the two entry tickets (about ARS 24,000 each for non-residents) plus one €9.99 purchase shared on one phone with a splitter or two phones with two purchases — and as long as you like. For two people alone: two tickets and forty minutes. The tour and the audio guide come out roughly level if the tour includes the tickets; the audio guide wins on flexibility and time, the tour on questions.

Common mistakes

Waiting at the gate for an English free tour that doesn't exist. Booking a 13:00 group tour in January and spending it in a queue at Evita's vault in the heat. Downloading the audio guide inside the cemetery on a signal that isn't there. Doing the tour, skipping the second lap, and realising on the plane that you never saw the boxer. And the classic: walking in unprepared, seeing Evita, and leaving.

The takeaway

Take a guide if you want a person. Take the audio guide if you want the cemetery. Take both if you can. Whatever you choose, be at the gate at 9:00 — the format matters less than the hour.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need a guide for Recoleta Cemetery?

Not to get in — it's fully open to independent visitors. But without some kind of guidance most people see Evita and little else. A map plus stories (a live guide or an audio guide) is what turns the maze into a visit.

Are there free tours of Recoleta Cemetery in English?

No. The City's free guided walks leave the gate every hour but are in Spanish only. English tours are paid (from roughly US$15–30 per person on GetYourGuide) or self-guided with an audio guide.

How much does a guided tour of Recoleta cost?

Small-group English tours typically run US$15–35 per person, often including the non-resident entry ticket; private tours from about US$60–100. A self-guided audio guide is a one-off €9.99 and doesn't include the ticket.

How long is a guided tour?

Most are 1.5 to 2 hours and cover 15–25 tombs at a fixed pace. The audio tour covers 22 stops in about 90 minutes, but you can take three hours or twenty minutes.

Can I combine a tour with an audio guide?

Yes, and it's the best of both: take the free Spanish tour or a paid English one for the chronology and the route, then go back around with the audio guide to the tombs you want to linger at.

Which is best with children?

Self-guided. Children rarely last a two-hour group tour among strangers; with an audio guide you can turn it into a symbol hunt, skip stops, and leave when the ice-cream call comes.
